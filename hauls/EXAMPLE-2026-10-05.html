Energy

1. Oil is back above $100, and the government expects it to stay high.

Brent, the world's benchmark crude, rose to about $101 a barrel on October 7. The US Energy Information Administration (EIA) now expects it to average $105 through the end of the year, $14 more than it forecast a month ago. Diesel is the tightest spot: East Coast stocks of diesel and heating oil were 32% below normal for September.

Sources: EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook (Oct 6) · Reuters, via The Straits Times (Oct 7)

2. A Gulf storm switched off a quarter of offshore oil.

Ahead of Tropical Storm Isaias, producers shut in about 511,619 barrels a day of oil, about 25% of Gulf offshore output, and about 16% of offshore gas, as of midday October 7. Shutting in means closing safety valves until the storm passes; it is a precaution, not damage.

Sources: Argus Media (Oct 7) · Marine Minerals Administration (Oct 6)

3. Google is paying to squeeze more power out of 11 existing reactors.

A 20-year deal with Constellation funds "uprates" (more output from plants that already run) at six plants in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey: about 890 megawatts of new power by 2032, with the first extra power expected by 2028.

Source: Constellation (Oct 6)

4. China connected another large reactor to the grid.

Tianwan 7, a Russian-designed reactor in Jiangsu province, sent its first electricity to the grid on October 8. Power will be raised in steps before commercial operation.

Source: World Nuclear News (Oct 8)

5. Poland's first offshore wind farm has all 76 turbines up.

Baltic Power, 1.1 gigawatts in the Baltic Sea, is on track to start commercial operation in 2026 and should make about 3% of Poland's electricity.

Source: Northland Power (Oct 7)

Across the economy

6. Manufacturing: a $6.6 billion submarine shipyard for Baltimore.

Anduril will put $3.7 billion of its own money into a shipyard at Sparrows Point, Maryland, and the Navy awarded a contract worth up to $2.9 billion. About 3,100 direct jobs; first operations targeted for 2030. The site was meant for wind turbine parts until US Wind gave up its lease this week.

Sources: Anduril (Oct 6) · Office of Governor Wes Moore (Oct 6) · Maryland Matters (Oct 7)

7. Policy: Iowa approved $1.36 billion in tax breaks for a $15 billion steel mill.

Lawmakers passed the bill in a one-day special session and the governor signed it the same night. The mill, led by Mesabi Metallics in Lee County, would employ at least 1,750 people and make steel with electric arc furnaces. Some lawmakers in both parties said it was rushed.

Source: Construction Dive (Oct 6)

8. Technology: an old Finnish paper mill becomes a $10 billion AI data center.

French company Sesterce plans a campus at the former Kaipola mill in Jämsä, Finland: 200 megawatts at first, 600 megawatts in phase two. It says it has an anchor customer and will add a megawatt of new renewable power to the grid for every megawatt it uses.

Source: DatacenterDynamics (Oct 8)
